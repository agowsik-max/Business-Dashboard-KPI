Subscription churn dashboard

Who leaves, how quickly, and how much monthly revenue leaves with them. Use the filters to slice by contract, tenure and payment method; every number, chart and the table below updates together.

KPI definitions

Churn rate
churned customers ÷ all customers in the current filter.
Retention rate
1 − churn rate, the share of customers still subscribed.
Revenue at risk
Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) from churned customers: Σ MonthlyCharges where Churn = Yes. Shown with its share of total MRR and its annualised value (×12).
Tenure
Average TenureMonths, reported separately for churned and retained customers.

Insight

Contract type separates leavers from stayers completely. All 7 month-to-month customers churned; none of the 8 customers on one-year or two-year contracts did.

Moving month-to-month customers onto a term contract is the largest retention lever in this sample.

ChurnedRetained

Insight

Risk is front-loaded. Every customer in their first 12 months churned (6 of 6), and the longest-tenured churner left at month 14.

Nobody past 25 months left. An onboarding push in the first year is where retention effort pays off.

Churn by payment method

ChurnedRetained

Insight

Bank transfer customers all stayed (3 of 3); UPI and debit card customers mostly left (3 of 4 and 2 of 2).

Payment method overlaps with contract type here: all UPI and debit churners were month-to-month, so treat this as a signal to investigate, not a cause.

Churn by subscription plan

ChurnedRetained

Insight

Churn is concentrated in the cheapest plan. Basic lost 5 of 7 customers, Pro 2 of 4, and Enterprise none.

This is why churners are 47% of customers but only 32% of monthly revenue.

Insight

$409.93 of $1,269.85 monthly revenue (32.3%) has churned, about $4,919 a year.

Five Basic churners account for $249.95 of that and two Pro churners for $159.98. A lost Pro account is worth 1.6× a lost Basic one, so Pro churn deserves attention despite the smaller count.
